# The ButtKicker as a telemetry display

**16 Aug 2026 · GT7 v1.70 · Porsche 911 RSR (991) '17, Gr.3 · packet format `C`**

Evidence: 278,034 frames — 40 laps, 77 minutes of the driver's own practice —
read through `Store` and replayed through the real derivers. Every number in
this document is either measured on the rig with him in the seat, or measured
off those laps. Where a number is neither, it says so.

---

## 0. The four findings that changed the design

**1. The wheelspin cue was a throttle-position readout.** The rear axle reads a
slip ratio of 1.032 across a lap and the threshold was 1.04. Split by throttle
the picture is unambiguous:

| throttle | 0–5% | 5–15% | 15–30% | 30–50% | 50–70% | 70–90% |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| median rear − front | 0.0002 | 0.0064 | 0.0180 | 0.0302 | 0.0442 | 0.0596 |

At a closed throttle the rear axle reads *exactly* what the front reads. The
offset is not a calibration artefact to subtract — it is genuine driven-wheel
slip, proportional to the torque going through the axle, and a tyre putting
power down is supposed to do it. So the reference is a **curve learned per
car**, not a constant, and wheelspin is what stands above it.

**2. GT7's ABS regulates; it does not pulse.** Front-axle slip under braking:

    p50 0.070   p75 0.135   p90 0.147   p95 0.152   p99 0.171   max 0.258

and it stays **above 0.10 for a median of 1292 ms at a time, 204 separate
times** across 40 laps. The lock detector carried a 220 ms attack added
specifically to reject an ABS pulse train — which does not exist. A 220 ms
attack climbs a 1292 ms plateau perfectly. The effect was reporting a lock-up
through most of every braking zone, exactly as reported from the seat ("ABS is
way too strong"), and the fix intended to stop it could not have.

**3. The road bed sat on top of the traction cue.** Wheel-spin at 82–108 Hz,
road rumble at 86–104 Hz. The bed is live for most of a lap and the limit cue
for a tenth of one, on one piston, summed into one signal. No gain fixes that:
raising the cue raises what it must beat once the limiter closes.

**4. The measurement instrument did not run.** `tools/rig_levels.py` had a
`SyntaxError` on line 208 and had never executed as committed. It has been
repaired and rebuilt to drive the real objects rather than a numpy
reimplementation of them.

## 1. Existing native architecture (Gate 1)

Traced end to end, no filename guessing.

    PS5 ──UDP 33740──▶ UDPListener (daemon thread, heartbeat 'C' to 33739 @1 Hz)
                          │ raw datagram
                          ▼
                     TelemetryBridge.on_packet          [UDP thread]
                          ├─ CaptureWriter (optional tee, pre-parse)
                          ├─ parse_packet ─ Salsa20, magic check, 368 B
                          ├─ LapRecorder.record_frame   (integrates lap distance)
                          ├─ ShiftBeep.update
                          ├─ SessionState.update ─▶ Qt signals
                          ├─ EffectDeriver.update ─▶ HapticsEngine.set_intensities
                          └─ WindCurve.update    ─▶ WindSim.set_output
                          ▼
                     HapticsEngine._wanted  (plain float32 array, no lock)
                          ▼ generation counter
                     _callback                          [PortAudio thread]
                          ├─ watchdog: counts FRAMES, not wall clock
                          ├─ HapticMix.render ─ voices, duck, DC block, limiter
                          └─ to_stereo ─▶ WASAPI shared ─▶ BKA-PRO ─▶ piston

Facts worth stating because they bound everything else:

- **Rate**: measured 59.88 Hz, not 60.0. The app assumes 60.0 for `dt`; the
  0.2% error is below anything a piston can express.
- **Thread boundary**: one array write. Nothing on the telemetry thread waits
  on a sound card. A raising haptic path is caught, logged and unwired for the
  session — an output may never cost a recorded lap.
- **Watchdog**: counts frames *inside the audio callback*. A Python timer
  cannot help when the Python side is what died; the sound card's clock is the
  only one still ticking.
- **Device**: matched by name, `strict=True` — never the default card, because
  a switched-off ButtKicker would otherwise route the road bed to his
  headphones.
- **Shared, not exclusive**: exclusive mode opens, reports a plausible latency
  and renders nothing (endpoint metered 0.0000).
- **Health**: `take_recent_peak()` (did *we* render?) against the Windows
  endpoint meter (did the *card* play it?). Loud in and nothing out is a dead
  transducer and nothing else looks like that.
- **Wind**: entirely separate path, its own worker thread and serial port.
  Untouched by this work.

**No second mixer was created.** Everything below is inside `HapticMix`.

## 2. Rig Frequency Response Register (Gate 2)

Measured 16 Aug 2026, nine equal-amplitude tones at −18 dBFS, one per run,
rated 0–3 by the driver in the seat. The endpoint metered exactly 0.125 for
every one, so the digital side was identical and every difference is the rig.

| Hz | rating | class | note |
|---|---|---|---|
| <25 | — | **DEAD** | amp's fixed 25 Hz low-cut, 12 dB/oct. 20 Hz at −18 dBFS felt as nothing. |
| 30 | 2.0 | MODERATE | |
| 40 | 3.0 | **STRONG / CLEAN** | most efficient frequency on the rig |
| 50 | 3.0 | **STRONG / CLEAN** | |
| 60 | 2.0 | MODERATE | |
| 70 | 1.0 | **DEAD (null)** | resonance null — seat/mount/chassis |
| 85 | 2.0 | MODERATE | |
| 100 | 2.0 | MODERATE | |
| 120 | 1.0 | WEAK | |
| 140 | 0.5 | WEAK | |
| >160 | — | **DEAD** | amp high-cut switch, set to 160 |

Two usable regions: **28–66 Hz** and **80–115 Hz**. This is a resonance
structure, not a rolloff — which refutes the 1/f² model three earlier rounds of
trims were sized against.

Reference level: **40 Hz at −6 dBFS**, amp at 35 of 50. Settled operating point
after a master of 2 tripped the amp's protection and needed a PC restart.

Nothing in this document replaces measured rig behaviour with convention.

## 5. Derived vehicle-state architecture (Gates 5–10)

All in `pitcrew/rig/vehicle.py`. Every state carries a confidence; every
threshold carries the percentile of his own driving it sits at.

### Rear traction (Gate 5)
`GRIPPED → APPROACHING_SLIP → USEFUL_SLIP → EXCESSIVE_WHEELSPIN →
SEVERE_TRACTION_LOSS`, plus `UNKNOWN` before the reference has settled.

- Input: rear-axle slip minus the **learned expected slip at this throttle**
  (four quantile trackers, q=0.35, interpolated between band centres).
- Reference is **blanked upward** while an event is running — a quantile
  converges on the quantile of the stream it is shown, and ten seconds of a
  held 12% slide moved it 0.042 and quartered the cue. Downward movement is
  always allowed: a reference that is too high hides events.
- **The front axle is the reference off the brakes and the road is the
  reference on them.** A locking front made the rear look like it was spinning
  — front 0.74 against rear 0.95 is a heavy stop, and the model called it a
  full-scale slide. Opposite correction, on the cue that matters most. Caught
  by a test, not by driving.
- False positives handled: differential (rear wheels report identically 75% of
  the time on this car — measured), gearshift (50 ms blanking), low speed
  (5 m/s gate), off-surface, single-frame noise (2-frame confirm).
- **Second witness**: sideslip, for the case wheel speed cannot see at all.
  Lift-off and trail-brake oversteer produce no wheelspin. The driver is told
  the larger of the two, because "the rear is going" is one message.

Measured over 40 laps: GRIPPED 74.4%, APPROACHING 13.1%, USEFUL 9.8%,
EXCESSIVE 2.2%, SEVERE 0.3%.

### Braking (Gate 6)
`FREE → STABLE → AT_LIMIT → LOCKED`, plus `REAR_UNSTABLE` and
`INCIPIENT_LOCK` (unloaded wheel).

- `AT_LIMIT` **is the ABS regulating**, at a deliberately modest level. A
  driver who can feel where the regulator starts working can brake to it. This
  is the single largest new piece of information in the system.
- `LOCKED` sits above a **learned plateau** (q=0.85 of the worst axle while the
  brakes are working) with a hard floor at 0.170. A peak-follower was tried and
  is wrong: it climbs to the session's largest excursion and silently switches
  the cue off. With ABS off there is no plateau and the floor governs.
- **The 220 ms attack is gone.** Removing it takes a fifth of a second out of
  the most time-critical cue in the system, and it was only ever there to
  reject a pulse train that does not exist.
- `REAR_UNSTABLE` is corroborated: a confirmed wheel-speed reversal (rare —
  3.08% of braking frames at any margin, in runs of one to two frames) **or**
  rotation while the brake is meaningfully applied. Wheel speed alone is too
  close to the noise to drive a critical cue.

Measured: FREE 85.0%, STABLE 7.3%, AT_LIMIT 7.0%, REAR_UNSTABLE 0.6%,
LOCKED 0.1%.

### Rotation (Gate 7)
Sideslip from the world velocity vector against the yaw rate — both in the base
packet, so it works on every format GT7 emits.

Validated on 40 laps by reconstructing the path heading from position over a
±83 ms stencil: **corr(yaw, path yaw) = −0.9985, slope −0.998**. So `angvel_y`
is the chassis yaw rate, the world path heading runs in the *opposite* sense,
and the residual is the sideslip rate. The sign is measured, not assumed, and
`_HeadingCheck` re-proves it continuously at runtime — a correlation that falls
away takes the cue with it, because a silently flipped sign would turn an
oversteer warning into a reward for it.

**Honest limit**: reconstructed from centimetre-quantised positions the
residual sits near its own noise floor — 0.06° on a straight against 0.18° of
real sideslip at the limit. That floor is an artefact of *what was recorded*,
not of the feed: live, the velocity vector is a float and needs no
differencing. Until a lap carrying world velocity has been recorded and
checked, this state is capped at MEDIUM confidence and attenuated to 0.80 where
it feeds the traction cue. `vel_x/y/z` are now recorded, so the next session
settles it.

### Tyre load and unload (Gate 9)
Suspension compression, per corner, as a z-score against a slowly-learned
neutral. **Sign measured, not assumed**: front 290.2 mm under heavy braking
against 284.7 cruising, rear 279.9 against 301.5, and corr(roll, signed lateral
g) = **−0.976**. Larger is more loaded. The four-corner sum correlates 0.861
with speed² — aerodynamic downforce showing up in the only channel that can see
it.

Never called tyre force. GT7 reports none, and CLAUDE.md 3.3 is explicit that
reporting one implies a measurement nobody made.

### Suspension and chassis (Gate 10)
Distinct and each with a reason to exist: normal movement → texture bed;
kerb → measured surface change, strike on the *edge* only; landing → the
*return* after lightness (a compression without flight before it is a bump);
compression → excursion toward the observed extreme (**not** called bottoming);
impact → a step in world velocity no engine could produce.

## 6. Frequency architecture (Gate 14)

The organising idea, because a tactile vocabulary has to be learnable without
being memorised: **low is the car, high is the contact patch.**

| Band | felt | Effect | Class | Input | Modulation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 28–34 | 1.8–2.4 | `engine` | BED | his RPM curve | — |
| 34–41 | 2.4–3.0 | `road` | BED | suspension velocity + surface | — |
| 40–50 | 3.0 | `brake_limit` | **CRITICAL** | brake state | **7→16 Hz, depth 0.55** |
| 50 | 3.0 | `driveline` | TRANSIENT | shift, limiter | — |
| 52–60 | 2.9–2.0 | `impact` | TRANSIENT | kerb, landing, compression, hit | — |
| 56–66 | 2.4–1.4 | `chassis_load` | STATE | lateral g | — (band-compensated) |
| 86–104 | 2.0–1.8 | `rear_traction` | **CRITICAL** | traction level | **5→14 Hz, depth 0.45** |
| — | — | `unload` | MODIFIER | how light the car is | ducks the background |

**Collisions are deliberate and resolved in time, not frequency.** Brake and
impact share 44–50 Hz; a brake cue is a pulse train lasting a braking zone and
an impact is a 120 ms one-shot. What the body cannot do is separate two
continuous rasps in the same band — which is precisely what the old placement
asked of it.

**Nothing crosses the 70 Hz null.** Nothing continuous shares the high band
with the traction cue. The two CRITICAL cues are an octave and a bit apart,
because confusing "the fronts are locking" with "the rear is going" produces
opposite corrections.

**Amplitude modulation is the second axis, and it was entirely unused.** At a
40–100 Hz carrier the receptors integrate rather than resolve, so 44 Hz and
52 Hz feel like one effect at two strengths. Flutter between 5 and 20 Hz is
discriminated well, and it is also what the events *feel* like: a locking tyre
judders, a spinning one rasps. Above ~20 Hz the modulation fuses with the
carrier and stops being a rate, which is why both ranges stop short of it.

**Band compensation** (new): an effect that climbs its band is divided by the
response at the frequency it is playing and multiplied by the response at its
centre, clamped to 0.6–1.8. `felt_trim` is a number for where an effect *sits*
and can do nothing for one that *moves* — chassis load spans 2.4 down to 1.4,
so loading the car harder used to raise the amplitude and lower the delivery at
the same time.

## 7. Gain, dynamic range, priority and ducking (Gates 15–16)

Four classes. Lower is more important.

| Class | Ducked by | Ducks | Ceiling |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 CRITICAL | **nothing** | background −15 dB | sustained (0.5) |
| 1 TRANSIENT | nothing | background −10 dB | reaches into the −3 dBFS headroom |
| 2 STATE | critical, transient, unload | — | sustained |
| 3 BED | critical, transient, unload | — | sustained |

- `DUCK_CRITICAL = 0.82` (≈15 dB), `DUCK_DEPTH = 0.70` (≈10 dB), attack 20 ms,
  release 180 ms. **The point is not to make the cue loud — it is to make it
  the only thing happening**, which is a much cheaper way to be noticed on a
  piston with a finite ceiling.
- **Two criticals at once**: they coincide on 0.14% of measured frames. The
  lesser is attenuated to 0.45 so one of them is clearly the message.
- **Unload adds nothing.** The background is pulled down 75%. A real car goes
  quiet over a crest; reproducing that costs no band, and it is the one cue
  here that cannot be masked, because it is not a signal.
- **One ceiling for every effect.** Split class ceilings were tried before and
  quietly rewrote the driver's balance; the headroom above the sustained
  ceiling belongs to the limiter and to brief peaks, not to a class.
- His gains are unchanged: 70.00 / 37.62 / 35.19 / 39.87 / 12.31 / 9.52. Every
  change is carried in `felt_trim`, so "X is too strong" has one number to
  change and it is obvious which.

Measured over 12 laps with the real chain:

| effect | class | Hz | median | p99 | peak | live |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| engine | BED | 28–34 | 0.0847 | 0.1034 | 0.1072 | 100% |
| road | BED | 34–41 | 0.0467 | 0.1968 | 0.2150 | 53% |
| brake_limit | CRITICAL | 40–50 | 0.0000 | 0.1896 | 0.2895 | 15% |
| driveline | TRANSIENT | 50 | 0.0000 | 0.1019 | 0.1566 | 12% |
| impact | TRANSIENT | 52–60 | 0.0000 | 0.1472 | 0.1934 | 4% |
| chassis_load | STATE | 56–66 | 0.0000 | 0.1768 | 0.2074 | 43% |
| rear_traction | CRITICAL | 86–104 | 0.0000 | 0.2766 | 0.5000 | 9% |

Background: median 1.00 of full, minimum 0.18. **Contrast, in felt strength**:
a shift is 2.4× the road bed under it (it was 0.9× before this work started);
a kerb strike 7.6×; the braking cue 38× the engine; the traction cue 21.9× the
road bed *that used to sit on top of it*.

**Note the median column.** Every cue except the two beds has a median of
zero. A limit cue that is on most of the lap is not a limit cue — that is why
`rear_traction` carries a threshold of 15: without it the channel was live for
84% of the lap at −20 dBFS, which is a second bed in the one band reserved for
limit cues.

## 8. Front tyre saturation — a documented omission (Gate 8)

**Not built, deliberately.** Correct omission beats fake information.

The obvious model is measured yaw against the yaw a neutral-steer car would
do. It needs a road-wheel angle. Fed the in-game rim at offset 296 instead — a
±π channel that saturates at full lock — it becomes a steering meter: an
earlier version of exactly this model correlated **0.991 with steering × speed**
and over-predicted yaw by 3.67×, sitting at full scale for 34–53% of every lap.

Attempting it properly on the recorded laps, the implied steer gain falls
monotonically with steering angle:

| \|steering_norm\| | 0.01–0.03 | 0.03–0.06 | 0.15–0.22 | 0.30–0.40 | 0.40–0.60 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| implied δ/sn | 0.185 | 0.171 | 0.137 | 0.085 | 0.074 |
| mean lateral g | 0.55 | 1.10 | 1.50 | 1.56 | 1.61 |

A factor of 2.5. Some of that is genuine tyre saturation and some is the
steering rack, and **with only the rim channel they cannot be separated** —
mean lateral g is already 1.10 g in the second band, so the linear range this
model needs barely exists in his driving.

Three things follow:

1. `road_wheel_rad` (offsets 352/356, already parsed, documented as "the angle
   a bicycle model wants") is **now recorded**. One session settles it.
2. Until then, no front cue. A front-saturation warning that is really a
   steering-angle meter would fire in every corner he takes properly.
3. Even once calibrated, the DD Extreme already carries front load, self-
   aligning torque and the moment it goes light, directly to his hands. The
   likely correct answer is a **severe-only** complementary cue, not an early
   warning — the wheel gets there first.

## 9. Latency (Gate 18)

| Stage | Cost | Note |
|---|---|---|
| GT7 → socket | ~16.7 ms | one frame at 59.88 Hz; not reducible |
| parse + recorder + state | <0.1 ms | |
| `VehicleModel` + 7 effects | **34 µs** | 0.21% of a frame |
| `set_intensities` | ~1 µs | one array write, no lock |
| callback wait | 0–21 ms | PortAudio chooses the block; ~10 ms mean |
| `HapticMix.render` (1024) | **312 µs** | 1.46% of the block's own duration |
| WASAPI shared → amp | ~10–20 ms | measured elsewhere, not by us |

**Total ≈ 40–60 ms**, dominated by things outside this code.

What *was* inside this code and has been removed:

- **220 ms** of lock-detector attack. The single largest latency saving
  available anywhere in the chain, and it bought nothing.
- Symmetric 20 ms smoothing on the critical cues → **6 ms attack, 50 ms
  release**. Attack and release are separate questions: a warning that arrives
  late is not a warning, and one that stops the instant the wheel grips
  chatters at its own threshold.
- Level flicker is handled by an **80 ms release envelope**, not by an attack
  filter. Measured: the traction excess crosses its onset in 6445 episodes of
  which the median is a *single frame*; a symmetric filter would have cost
  latency in both directions to fix a problem that only exists in one.

Filters by use case: engine and road tolerate the 20 ms default. The two
CRITICAL cues do not and no longer carry it.

## 10. Observability (Gate 22)

Two halves, because the question has two halves.

`EffectDeriver.explain()` — the car: every state, its level, its confidence,
its witness, the learned reference, the lock threshold, the raw inputs.

`HapticMix.explain()` — the mix: per effect, raw → shaped → base gain →
ducked-by → final amplitude → frequency being played → what the rig delivers
there. Built outside the callback; the callback writes into preallocated
arrays, because allocating in an audio callback is the one thing not to do.

Both are logged periodically by `PitCrewController._log_haptic_state`, only for
effects actually contributing:

    mix: road 0.041@37Hz -18% · rear_traction 0.288@97Hz · engine 0.019@31Hz -18%
    car: traction EXCESSIVE_WHEELSPIN 0.61 (wheel, high) · brake FREE 0.00 (None)
         · rotation ROTATING 0.12 (medium) · unload 0.00

"What exactly caused that vibration in turn four" is answerable an hour later.

## 11. Hardware safety (Gate 23)

- Soft knee at −3 dBFS (`TRANSIENT_CEILING`), hard clip at −1 dBFS. Not for
  damage — the amp has a thermal cutout — but because the BKA-PRO's DC-protect
  **stops the unit until it is reset**, which mid-race means no haptics for the
  rest of it.
- Limiter engagements are counted. Reaching it routinely means the mix is
  wrong, not that the limiter is working.
- Duty cycle is the real constraint: the 150 W rating assumes one-third. The
  master gain drives duty cycle and the amp's own knob does not, which is why
  "not strong enough" is answered with the knob (at 35 of 50 — 3 dB spare).
- DC blocker at 5 Hz, ramped between blocks so the correction is not itself a
  discontinuity.
- Watchdog fades to silence in 150 ms after 200 ms of telemetry silence. A
  stuck tone at 150 W is the failure mode that matters most.
- Nothing is synthesised below 25 Hz: excursion spent for no output, and
  excursion is what bottoms a piston.

Driver discomfort is not the safety mechanism anywhere.

## 12. Test evidence (Gate 24)

`1540 passed, 1 skipped` — full suite, no hardware.

New: **`test_vehicle.py`, 27 tests**, every one a driving situation described
in packets rather than an assertion about arithmetic. Coverage includes:
ordinary acceleration is not wheelspin; the same slip at a different throttle
is a different claim; the reference does not learn the wheelspin; a gearshift
is not wheelspin; a single frame of noise is not a state; the ABS regulating is
not a lock; a real lock goes past the plateau; the lock threshold cannot be
learned away; the rear locking harder than the front is its own state; an
unloaded wheel is not a lock; rotation is unknown until the channels have been
checked; channels that stop agreeing take the cue with them; the rear coming
round off the throttle still reaches the driver; bigger suspension numbers mean
more load; a crest reads as going light; a landing is the return and not the
flight; a kerb from grass is not a strike.

Rewritten: **`test_synth.py`, 36 tests** — the two limit cues are an octave
apart; nothing continuous shares the high band; a pulsed effect actually pulses
the *signal*; band compensation keeps felt strength flat; a limit cue is never
ducked by anything; two limit cues leave one in charge; going light pulls the
background down and adds nothing; the mix can say what it just did.

Rewritten: **`test_effects.py`, 28 tests** — including
`test_the_two_critical_channels_stay_separate`, which is the test that caught
the front-lock-reads-as-rear-spin defect described in §5.

Instrument: `tools/rig_levels.py` rebuilt to drive the **real** derivers frame
by frame. The previous version reimplemented them in numpy, which is how it
came to be measuring a slightly different system from the one being driven.

## 14. What is still open

1. **Validate the sideslip model** against a lap recorded with `vel_x/y/z`.
   Until then the rotation cue is MEDIUM and attenuated. One session.
2. **Calibrate the road-wheel angle** from `road_wheel_rad` and decide whether
   a severe-only front cue earns its place. One session.
3. **Blind-recognition UAT** (§15). Nothing here proves the driver can *name*
   a cue, only that it is present, distinct and correctly derived.
4. **Per-car reference convergence** has been replayed on one car. The design

   value — but that is an argument, not a measurement, until a second car has
   been driven.
5. **Pace evidence** (Gate 26). Lap-time variance, throttle consistency and

## 15. Hardware UAT procedure (Gate 25)

Amp at **35**, master **1.0**. If it feels weak, the knob is the right answer
first — the master drives the limiter and the duty cycle and the knob drives
neither.

### A. Confirm the response has not moved (10 min)

    python tools/haptics_bench.py band --hz 40
    python tools/haptics_bench.py band --hz 50
    python tools/haptics_bench.py band --hz 70
    python tools/haptics_bench.py band --hz 95

Expect strong / strong / almost nothing / moderate. **If 70 Hz is now strong or
40 Hz is now weak, stop** — the mounts have moved and every placement in §6
needs re-deriving.

### B. Learn each cue in isolation (20 min)

Each one twice: once held, once swept, eyes closed.

    python tools/haptics_bench.py cue rear_traction --sweep
    python tools/haptics_bench.py cue brake_limit --sweep
    python tools/haptics_bench.py cue impact --level 0.9
    python tools/haptics_bench.py cue driveline --level 0.8
    python tools/haptics_bench.py cue road --level 0.5
    python tools/haptics_bench.py cue chassis_load --sweep
    python tools/haptics_bench.py cue engine --level 0.6

Record for each: felt at all? Does the sweep read as *more* rather than
*different*? Does the pulse rate on the two critical cues read as urgency?

### C. Separability — the question that actually matters (20 min)

    python tools/haptics_bench.py pair rear_traction road
    python tools/haptics_bench.py pair brake_limit road
    python tools/haptics_bench.py pair impact road
    python tools/haptics_bench.py pair brake_limit chassis_load
    python tools/haptics_bench.py pair rear_traction chassis_load

Each plays the background alone, then the cue on top. The question is **not**
whether the second one is loud. It is whether it could be named without being
told which it was.

> **Result, 16 Aug 2026, driver in the seat:** B and C pass. The cues "feel
> different and distinguishable" on the bench. That is the separability
> question, and it is the one no amount of replay could have answered - two
> continuous rasps in the same twelve hertz was the failure this whole
> spectral plan was built to avoid, and the body confirms the plan works.
>
> Still unrun: D (on track), E (blind recognition) and F (fatigue over a
> stint). Bench separability with one cue at a time and full attention is a
> weaker claim than naming a cue at racing speed with a helmet on, and the
> difference between those two is exactly where a tactile vocabulary is won
> or lost.

### D. On track, one category at a time (2 x 20 min)

Set every `felt_trim` but one to a low value and drive five laps. Order:
`brake_limit`, then `rear_traction`, then the rest together. Note where each
fires against where you expected it. `_log_haptic_state` writes the answer to
the log every ten seconds, so a disagreement is checkable rather than
remembered.

### E. Blind recognition (20 min, needs a second person or a scripted run)

Five events, called out loud as they are felt, without looking at anything:

1. incipient wheelspin (`APPROACHING`/`USEFUL`)
2. excessive wheelspin (`EXCESSIVE`/`SEVERE`)
3. ABS regulating (`AT_LIMIT`)
4. a genuine lock (`LOCKED`)
5. rear instability under braking (`REAR_UNSTABLE`)

Score against `explain()` in the log. **Below about 80% on 1 vs 2 or 3 vs 4,
the modulation rates are the thing to change** — not the gains. Confusing 4
with 5 means the rear-instability state needs its own signature rather than
sharing the braking cue's.

### F. Everything on, race pace (30 min)

The failure to look for is fatigue: after twenty minutes, is the bed still
informative or has it become wallpaper? If the latter, the beds come down,
not the cues up.
