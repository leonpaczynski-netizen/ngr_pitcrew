# Rig sweep after the vertical remount — 12 Sep 2026

Driver in the seat, endpoint verified at rate first. Measured with
`tools/haptics_bench.py band --shared`, one tone per invocation.
Procedure: `docs/RIG-RETUNE_REMOUNT.md`.

**Nothing in `transducer.py` has been changed on the strength of this yet** —
the configuration is not settled (amp moved 35 -> 25 mid-session, and whether
the mount stays vertical is an open decision). These are measurements, not a
new calibration.

---

## Pre-flight

`endpoint_rate.py`, silence, ten seconds, single-threaded:

    delivered   48103 f/s   (100.2% of nominal)
    blocks      100.2 /s    (480 frames each)
    DAC clock   48079 f/s

Healthy. Not the ~30.5k wedge. Every tone below metered 0.124-0.125 at
-18 dBFS, so the digital side was identical throughout and every difference
is the rig.

**Trap found in the instrument:** `haptics_bench band` defaults to EXCLUSIVE
(`exclusive = not args.shared`), and exclusive on this device opens, reports a
sensible latency and renders nothing. Every tone would have been silent and
rated 0. `--shared` was passed on every tone. The default contradicts
`transducer.EXCLUSIVE = False` and should be flipped — deliberately NOT changed
mid-session, so the instrument stayed fixed while measuring with it.

Control: 50 Hz re-run at the end of the sweep, came back 3 — the run stands.
60 Hz confirmed above 3 by scale-free paired comparison against 50 (driver:
"60 is stronger"), so the off-scale 4 is not a scale artefact.

**A rating of 4 was volunteered because the 0-3 scale ran out.** An earlier
"4" for 50 Hz was withdrawn by the driver to 3 on reflection.

### What this says

- **The 70 Hz null is gone.** `FELT_NULL_HZ = 70.0` is wrong. It was the seat
  brackets' antiresonance, not a property of the transducer.
- **The top end is alive.** 120 (1 -> 2) and 140 (0.5 -> 2). "Nothing above
  120" is wrong.
- **Flat at 2 from 70 to 140**, with a single sharp peak at 50-60.
- The old 40-55 peak has gone; 40 dropped to 2 while 50 held 3 and 60 went
  off-scale. The peak did not flatten, it **moved up and narrowed onto 60**.
- 60 Hz is the transducer's own reaction-mass resonance, previously masked by
  the compliant brackets and now exposed by the stiff path.

## 2. Knock — the finding that dominates everything

Knock is **inside the unit**, located by hand against bracket, RHS and seat
shell.

### Amp 35

| Hz | level | result |
|---|---|---|
| 30, 40, 50 | -18 | no knock |
| 60 | -24 | no knock |
| 60 | -18 | slight knock |
| 40 | -12 | knock, felt 3-4 |
| 85 | -6 | bad knock, very strong |
| 100 | -6 | knock, "more sound than vibration" |
| 120 | -6 | **no knock**, felt 3 |

Approximate knock thresholds: **60 Hz ~ -21 dBFS, 40 Hz ~ -15 dBFS**, 85 and
100 below -6, 120 above -6.

### Amp 25

| Hz | level | result |
|---|---|---|
| 40 | -6 | no knock, felt 2 |
| 85 | -6 | slight knock, felt 3 |

### Two distinct mechanisms, not one — driver's ear, confirmed acoustically

- **40-60 Hz: a dull thud, sharp level threshold (6 dB between clean and
  knocking).** End-stop knock. Mounting vertically parks the reaction mass
  off-centre under gravity, so travel in one direction is reduced and the
  resonance — where excursion peaks — reaches the stop first.
- **85-100 Hz: a buzzier rattle, also inside the unit.** A different mechanism,
  and it does not fit the end-stop story: 85 is consistently worse than 40
  despite sitting further above the resonance, where excursion should be lower.
  Cause not established.

**The rattle has NO horizontal control.** 85 Hz was only ever played at
-18 dBFS in August; nobody ran it at -6 on the old mount. It may predate the
remount and simply never have been excited, because the compliant brackets
delivered little at 85. Do not record it as a regression.

## 2a. The acoustic measurement — and three failed instruments first

Recorded while driving a fixed sequence (marker, then 40/60/85/100/120 at
-6 dBFS, amp 25, 5 s each with 2 s gaps). `scratchpad/knock_sequence.py`,
`play_and_measure.py`.

**Three instruments failed before one worked, and each failure is worth
keeping:**

1. **JBL mic jack: -71 dBFS.** Nothing plugged into it. Looked like a working
   endpoint in every enumeration.
2. **A mic endpoint can hand back EXACT ZEROS for seconds after the stream
   opens**, which is indistinguishable from a dead microphone. Any record-then-
   play harness must wait for real samples before driving anything.
3. **Laptop mic array failed its negative control.** 120 Hz — clean by ear —
   read crest 41 / kurtosis 489 at ten times the level of the other runs.
   Beamforming, AGC and noise suppression reshape exactly the transients being
   measured. Everything it produced was discarded, including a result that
   agreed with the hypothesis at the time.
4. **The USB mic has a NOISE GATE.** Its "silence" read -126 to -149 dBFS,
   which is not a room. Measuring each tone against the gap either side of it
   was therefore measuring the depth of the gate, not the knock. **The gate is
   open while a tone plays**, so tone-to-tone comparison is valid and
   tone-to-silence is not.

**What finally worked: normalise each tone against its OWN fundamental**, in
one fixed band (>800 Hz, above every fundamental and its first five harmonics
so all five tones share a band).

| tone | fundamental dB | >800 Hz dB | hi/fund | crest | kurtosis |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 40 | 61.9 | 38.2 | -23.8 | 6.12 | 4.85 |
| **60** | **91.5** | 73.4 | **-18.1** | 9.49 | **14.69** |
| 85 | 73.7 | 53.6 | **-20.1** | 10.12 | **4.57** |
| 100 | 64.7 | 27.7 | -37.0 | 18.14 | 5.81 |
| 120 | 75.7 | 40.5 | -35.2 | 25.29 | 14.83 |

**Two independent validations, which is why this table is usable and the
earlier ones were not:**

- **120 Hz measures clean** (-35.2, second lowest). The negative control that
  failed twice now passes.
- **60 Hz has the loudest fundamental by 18 dB**, corroborating the driver's
  off-scale "4" from the seat by a completely separate route.

**The discrimination, and it matches the driver's ear exactly:**

- **60 Hz — broadband AND impulsive** (kurtosis 14.7). Energy in discrete
  spikes. End-stop knock. The driver's "dull thud".
- **85 Hz — broadband but NOT impulsive** (kurtosis 4.6, the lowest in the
  set). The same excess noise spread continuously rather than spiked. A buzz.
  The driver's "buzzier rattle".

**The driver's report was right and the instrument was wrong twice before it
was right once.** An intermediate "it is all one mechanism" reading, taken off
the laptop array, was retracted. Standing rule 1 held: the report is the
evidence, the acoustics are corroboration.

**Consequence:** the 85-100 buzz is a SEPARATE fault from the travel problem,
not the same one heard at a faster repetition rate. It may have its own fix.

## 3. The one regression that IS controlled

August reference: **40 Hz, -6 dBFS, amp 50 — "very strong, no knock."**
Today: **40 Hz knocks at -12 dBFS with amp 35**, a lower drive than that
reference.

Same unit, same amp, same electronics, same digital levels. The mount is the
only variable. **Call it 9-12 dB of low-band headroom lost.**

At amp 25 the reference condition (40 Hz, -6) is clean but reads **2**, where
the August reference was "very strong". Headroom was bought with strength.

## 4. Where this leaves the rig

`SUSTAINED_CEILING` 0.5 (-6 dBFS) and `TRANSIENT_CEILING` 0.71 (-3 dBFS) are
**not reachable across most of the band** at amp 35. At amp 25, -6 dBFS is
clean at 40 Hz but still rattles at 85.

Six of the seven effects live in 28-66 Hz: engine 28-34, road 34-41,
brake_limit 40-50, driveline 50, impact 52-60, chassis_load 56-66. Only
rear_traction (86-104) sits outside it — and 85-100 is the rattle band.

**Open decision, not settled at the rig:**

1. **Stay vertical at a lower amp.** ~25, rebuild the spectral plan around a
   52-70 no-go band, and establish a new calibration reference. Costs absolute
   strength; keeps the flat response, the filled null and the live top end.
2. **Go back horizontal.** Restores the centred mass and full travel, and the
   August reference with it. Costs the flat response, the 70 Hz fill and
   everything above 120.

The gravity offset is intrinsic to mounting this unit vertically. Turning it
the other way up does not help — the offset is the same magnitude, just toward
the other end stop.

## Method notes for next time

- The **control worked and was needed**: 50 Hz came back 4 on re-run before the
  driver corrected it to 3. Without a control that drift is invisible.
- **Paired comparison beats absolute rating** once the scale has been broken.
  "Stronger, same or weaker than the last one" needs no stable scale.
- An off-scale answer is a finding, not an error — but confirm which it is
  rather than recording it.
- Ask where a noise lives (unit / bracket / frame / seat) before theorising
  about what it is. It ruled out a five-minute spanner fix here.
