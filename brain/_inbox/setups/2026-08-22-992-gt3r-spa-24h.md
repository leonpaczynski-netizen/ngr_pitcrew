# 0. The headline

**This is a cold start, and the sheet is honest about it.** There is no lap, no
session and no telemetry for this car or this circuit anywhere in the database —
`sessions` for event 4 is empty and no Spa lap has ever been recorded. There is
also **no car profile for the 992 GT3 R in the knowledge base**; `07` §9 still
carries *"whether a Porsche 911 GT3 R exists in GT7"* as **⚠️ Unverified**. It
exists, it is in the garage, and this is the first sheet ever issued for it.

So every value below is derived from three things and nothing else:

1. **The car's own slider ranges**, read off the screen today on v1.71 — the only
   measured input this sheet has.
2. **Spa's demands** (`05` §1.4), which are circuit geometry and do not patch.
3. **Your baseline philosophy** (`08` B1), anchored in absolutes rather than
   percent-of-range, because 1.71 moved the ranges.

**Three things to know before you drive it:**

1. **⚠️ This is an RR car, and it is NOT the RSR.** The RSR is **MR** — `07` §0.1
   is explicit that the 991 RSR is the first mid-engined 911 and that the MR row
   must be used for it. The 992 GT3 R is genuinely rear-engined, and the app
   agrees (`cars.drivetrain = 'RR'`, id 449). **Do not carry RSR numbers across.**
   The diff in particular is set differently below, and deliberately.
2. **⭐ Your range reading closed an open knowledge-base question — see §6.** It is
   the second Gr.3 car re-read on 1.71 and it makes the LSD change look fleet-wide
   rather than car-specific.
3. **⚠️ The gearbox is not on this sheet, and that is deliberate — see §3.**
   Nothing is known about this car's ratios, its limiter or its Vmax. Issuing gear
   numbers would be inventing them.

# 4. The three judgement calls, stated rather than buried

## 4.1 Compound — Racing Hard for practice, and the reason is measurement hygiene

**RH is not a race recommendation. It is the compound that lets you measure.**
`17` §0 used RH deliberately *"so wear would not confound the grip measurement"* and
it worked.

**The race compound is genuinely open**, and the Monza precedent says so loudly:
RM turned out ~2.0 s/lap faster there with a crossover around 11–14 laps, and that
finding rested on **one gauge reading**. Do not assume the same holds here.

> **⛔ I am not issuing a stint plan or a stop count, and I want to be explicit
> about why.** `05` §1.4 puts Spa at **12–16 laps at 1×** before the front-left
> costs two tenths. Dividing by 3 for the multiplier gives 4–5 laps, which would
> mean roughly ten stops in a 120-minute race — an implausible answer that shows
> the conversion is not safe to make. **`CLAUDE.md` §5.2 forbids exactly that
> silent conversion**, multiplier linearity is assumed rather than proven, and
> **tyre wear is completely unmeasured on v1.71 on any car** (`17` §8, fifth session
> running). Four unknowns stacked on one estimate is not a plan. **The gauge
> readings from practice are what settle it.**

## 4.2 ⚠️ Brake balance — your standing rule and the track reference disagree

**They are not reconciled here, because the disagreement is the finding.**

| Source | Says |
|---|---|
| **Your standing rule** (`brain/driver.md`, non-negotiable) | **Never move brake balance forward.** Rear stability comes from LSD braking sensitivity instead. `02` §7.3 calls this *"one of the better-validated claims in the knowledge base"* — four sessions, two cars, including a race won from P5. |
| **`05` §1.4, Spa** | *"Brake bias: one to two clicks forward. The downhill entries at La Source and Rivage set the requirement. A rearward bias will bite you at Rivage specifically."* |
| **`02` §7.3, RR row** | −1 to −2 (front) — *"prevent rear lock-up on an already-loose entry, same as MR but more so."* |

**The sheet ships at 0, because it is your rule and a cold-start sheet is not where
a standing rule gets overturned.** The structural answer is already applied: **LSD
braking is at 30**, the highest number on the diff, which is the RR tool for
precisely the entry that Rivage threatens.

> **But this is the event that tests the rule properly, and it should be tested
> rather than assumed.** Two independent things line up: Rivage is a downhill,
> tightening, off-camber stop and `05` names it specifically; and **1.71 adjusted
> ABS *"slip ratio control and cornering brake behaviour"*** — `02` §7.3 says that
> change *"is exactly what would test it. Re-confirm early."* Brake balance is
> adjustable live on the MFD, so this costs nothing: **if Rivage bites, take one
> click of front on the MFD for one lap and see.** That is a measurement, not a
> setup change, and it does not touch the sheet.

## 4.3 TCS and the weather

**The event declares TCS 0 and the sheet matches it.** Two things worth holding in
mind rather than acting on now:

- `08` B1's standing position is **TCS 0 for quali, 1 for race** — *"TCS 1 costs
  near-nothing and catches the worst exits."* ⚠️ That price was measured on v1.70;
  **1.71 "optimised" TCS intervention and the new price is unknown** (`16` Job 7).
- **Weather is `changeable` with `rain_possible = 1`, and the layout is rain-capable.**
  On an RR car in the wet, TCS 1 is the cheapest insurance available and it is a
  pause-menu change. **Decide it in the race, not now.**

# 5. What to test first — in order, one change per run, three clean laps

## ⭐ 1. LSD acceleration sensitivity — sweep it, do not walk up from 18

**The highest-value test on this car, and the reason 18 is written as a bracket.**
It is the same test `17` §9 and Rev C §6 nominate as the top priority on the RSR,
and here it is worse, because there is no prior value to walk away from.

**Four things pull in different directions:**

| Force | Direction |
|---|---|
| 1.71 cut driven-wheel slip under power by **40–60%** (`17` §1) — the wheelspin this parameter manages has largely gone | **up** |
| The range doubled: **5–60 → 0–100**. There is territory above that never existed | **up** |
| `05` §1.4: *"one of the few tracks where erring toward more acceleration lock is correct, because both traction exits feed straights."* **La Source exit is the single most consequential traction event on any circuit in GT7** | **up** |
| `08` A5: run it **low and prove you need more**. A locked diff pushes mid-corner *and* lets go of both wheels at once | **down** |

**Sweep 8 / 18 / 35 / 60.** Do not assume the optimum is near 18 — nothing supports
that. **Record per setting:** your report verbatim first, then median of three clean
laps, **rear slip ratio at full throttle in 2nd and 3rd**, and **La Source exit
speed at the pit-wall line**.

> **Watch for a flat response between 35 and 60.** On the RSR that would mean the
> parameter has gone insensitive, which `17` calls a bigger finding than any
> particular value. A second car showing it would make it a property of 1.71.

## 2. Ten laps on RH at 3×, gauge read at laps 3, 6 and 10

**The only thing that can change the race plan** (§4.1), and it is the fifth session
running that GT7's wear question has gone untouched on v1.71. `tools/read_hud_wear.py`
reads the gauge off OBS to 0.5% and needs nothing but running.

**Two things come free with it:**
- **Confirm the event's tyre wear actually reads 3×.** Thirty seconds, never once
  done on any event, and the entire baseline rests on it. Rev C flagged this twice.
- **Which corner goes first.** `05` §1.4 says Spa is **markedly left-side biased** and
  the **front-left** is the stint limiter, because Raidillon, Pouhon, Stavelot and
  Blanchimont are all sustained rights. **Your own history says rear-left** — but
  `driver.md` now records that 5 of 45 readings had a front worst, *all of them
  early-stint*. **A front-left-worst reading at Spa would be the first time
  circuit geometry and your wear signature actually agree**, and it would be worth
  recording as such.

## 3. Camber A/B — 0.8 / 1.2 / 1.8, front only

**Held off the sheet on purpose (§2.1) and it should be resolved by measurement.**
1.71 reworked per-car steering geometry, so the reason camber was pinned at 1.2 —
GT7 taxing it against braking and traction, your two strengths — rests on a model
that was re-authored. Spa's sustained fast rights are the strongest case for more
front camber anywhere in the league calendar, and the front-left is the tyre that
decides the stint. **This is `08` B1's Job 5 A/B, on the circuit where it matters
most.**

## 4. Ride height at Eau Rouge, then Pouhon

`05` gives the procedure and the order. **If the car ever bangs and then refuses to
steer, stop and raise it** — that is arch contact, and whether the 1.49 bottoming
problem survived 1.71 is still [UNKNOWN].
