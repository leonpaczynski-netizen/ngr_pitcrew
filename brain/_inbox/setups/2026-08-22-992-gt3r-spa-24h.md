# 3. ⚠️ Gearing — not settable, and that closes the question rather than deferring it

**BoP locks the transmission. `top`, `fg` and `gears` are unset on this sheet and
they stay unset — they are not missing data waiting to be filled in.** The app
stores them as absent rather than zero, which reads honestly as *not entered*
instead of as a real value (`sheet.py`: *"a value that was never entered is `None`,
not `0`"*).

**Spa's gearing advice in `05` §1.4 is therefore void for this event** — *"gear for
slipstream, 6th tops out with a tow at the Les Combes board, 4th must cover Pouhon,
5th must cover Blanchimont."* All of it presumes an adjustable gearbox. **Ignore it
here.** It stays true for the no-BoP rounds.

## 3.1 But still record the ratios — for a different reason

**The derivation is dead. The measurement is not, and it now matters more, because
with the gearbox fixed the only remaining lever on it is where you shift.**

1. **Photograph the transmission screen** — six ratios and the final drive. They go
   in the app's `gears` field once read. `17` §5 is the precedent: byte-identical
   ratios independently confirmed nothing else had changed across the patch.
2. **The limiter**, from any full-throttle upshift.
3. **Vmax down Kemmel in clean air**, and the rpm at which it arrives. Strategy
   still wants this even though gearing cannot act on it — it sets the slipstream
   economy on a circuit where `05` says *"a car 1.5 s/lap slower can hold position"*.

**Then the short-shift work becomes the whole transmission job.** `17` §4 measured
**−21.6% fuel for ~+0.5 s/lap** on v1.71, and your own optimum is ~1200 rpm below
the limiter. On a 120-minute race that is the lever that removes a stop — and
`sheet.py` keeps `shift_rpm` **per sheet**, keyed by gear, precisely because a shift
point is a property of the gearbox. **A locked gearbox means one table, measured
once, good for the whole event.**

> **⚠️ One caution carried straight from `17` §2.** 1.71 dropped the RSR's measured

> pre-patch counterpart — a baseline, comparable to nothing. Do not read a "slow
> car" out of it.

> **✅ And the `top` range worry in §1.1 stops mattering for now.** It was flagged

# 4. The three judgement calls, stated rather than buried

## 4.1 Compound — Racing Hard for practice, and the reason is measurement hygiene

**RH is not a race recommendation. It is the compound that lets you measure.**
`17` §0 used RH deliberately *"so wear would not confound the grip measurement"* and
it worked.

**The race compound is genuinely open**, and the Monza precedent says so loudly:
RM turned out ~2.0 s/lap faster there with a crossover around 11–14 laps, and that
finding rested on **one gauge reading**. Do not assume the same holds here.

> **⛔ I am not issuing a stint plan or a stop count, and I want to be explicit
> about why.** `05` §1.4 puts Spa at **12–16 laps at 1×** before the front-left
> costs two tenths. Dividing by 3 for the multiplier gives 4–5 laps, which would
> mean roughly ten stops in a 120-minute race — an implausible answer that shows
> the conversion is not safe to make. **`CLAUDE.md` §5.2 forbids exactly that
> silent conversion**, multiplier linearity is assumed rather than proven, and
> **tyre wear is completely unmeasured on v1.71 on any car** (`17` §8, fifth session
> running). Four unknowns stacked on one estimate is not a plan. **The gauge
> readings from practice are what settle it.**

## 4.2 ⚠️ Brake balance — your standing rule and the track reference disagree

**They are not reconciled here, because the disagreement is the finding.**

| Source | Says |
|---|---|
| **Your standing rule** (`brain/driver.md`, non-negotiable) | **Never move brake balance forward.** Rear stability comes from LSD braking sensitivity instead. `02` §7.3 calls this *"one of the better-validated claims in the knowledge base"* — four sessions, two cars, including a race won from P5. |
| **`05` §1.4, Spa** | *"Brake bias: one to two clicks forward. The downhill entries at La Source and Rivage set the requirement. A rearward bias will bite you at Rivage specifically."* |
| **`02` §7.3, RR row** | −1 to −2 (front) — *"prevent rear lock-up on an already-loose entry, same as MR but more so."* |

**The sheet ships at 0, because it is your rule and a cold-start sheet is not where
a standing rule gets overturned.** The structural answer is already applied: **LSD
braking is at 30**, the highest number on the diff, which is the RR tool for
precisely the entry that Rivage threatens.

## 4.2.1 ⭐ RESOLVED — the sweep was run 22 Aug, and the rule holds

**Three stints, 0 / −5 / +5, fresh RH and full fuel on each — a properly
controlled A/B.** Sessions 61, 62, 63.

**⛔ Lap time could not decide it, and never could have.** Best laps were
**2:27.153 / 2:27.393 / 2:27.766** — a **0.61 s span across the entire ±5 range**,
against a ~0.9 s noise floor. Entry rotation, apex speed and braking distance were
all unresolvable too: within-condition scatter swamped between-condition
differences. **The braking zones decided it instead.**

### What the telemetry resolved

**Rear-minus-front wheel slip under braking** — how much harder the fronts work
than the rears, the direct signature of the parameter:

| | La Source (T1) | Bus Stop (T19) |
|---|---|---|
| **BB −5** | **+0.0868** | **+0.0804** |
| **BB 0** | +0.0458 | +0.0571 |
| **BB +5** | +0.0300 | +0.0300 |

**Perfectly ordered, no overlap, replicated independently in two corners.**

> **⭐ The sign convention is now measured in-house.** `08` B3 called brake balance
> *"the single most commonly inverted parameter in GT7 advice"* and rested the
> direction on three agreeing sources. **Negative really is more front** — the
> fronts demonstrably do more work at −5, on this car, on v1.71.

**Peak deceleration**, per lap, with BB 0's learning lap identified and excluded:

| | La Source | Bus Stop |
|---|---|---|
| **BB −5** | **2.53 / 2.50** | **2.50 / 2.62** |
| BB 0 (clean lap only) | 2.44 | 2.44 |
| BB +5 | 2.44 / 2.44 | 2.50 / 2.46 |

**−5 stops hardest, by ~0.08 g. BB 0 and BB +5 are indistinguishable.**
⚠️ BB 0's two-lap mean was contaminated: it was the first session and lap 2 was his
first clean lap at the circuit. **The axle-split metric was immune to this** — it is
a distribution, not a magnitude — **which is why it resolved and peak g nearly did
not.** Worth remembering when designing the next test.

### ⭐ What the driver reported, which is what actually decided it

> **−5:** *"harder to rotate on entry as fronts are doing more work slowing the car"*
> **+5:** *"way more rotation and easier to hook the apex up"*

**This is the standing rule's own stated mechanism, felt and confirmed.** The rule
exists because front bias costs entry rotation, and deep trail-braking for rotation
is the technique. **He moved the bias forward and lost exactly what the rule says
he would.** The telemetry could not see it; he could. `04`'s first rule, working as
designed — the report is primary and the telemetry corroborates.

### The conclusion, and it is not the one `05` predicts

| | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **`05` §1.4's "brake bias one to two clicks forward" at Spa** | **⛔ REFUTED for this driver on this car.** Forward buys 0.08 g of stopping and costs the rotation his technique is built on. Recorded as a correction to `05` for this garage, not as a general one — the advice may be right for a driver who does not trail-brake. |
| **The standing rule "never move brake balance forward"** | ✅ **Vindicated on its own grounds**, for the first time by direct test rather than by precedent. |
| **⭐ BB +5** | **A legitimate race candidate.** It costs *nothing* in braking against neutral and buys real rotation. |

### ⭐ Why +5 is free, and the one thing to watch

**At BB 0 the rear axle was nowhere near its limit** — rear slip sat at 0.949–0.967
while the fronts were down at 0.892–0.931. **There was unused rear braking capacity,
and +5 spends it on rotation at no cost in stopping power.** That is the mechanism,
and it is why the trade came out one-sided.

> **⚠️ The one caution, and it is about the stint rather than the lap.** +5 makes the
> rears the hardest-working axle under braking (0.945–0.950) — and the Stavelot spin
> showed the rear axle is *also* marginal at high lateral load under power. **Two
> different phases, same axle.** The spin was not a braking event (brakes untouched,
> full throttle) so it is not evidence against +5 — but it does mean the rear is the
> limiting axle in both phases, and **as the rears wear through a stint, +5 gets
> worse.**
>
> **⭐ And this is where +5 turns out to be tactically ideal.** `02` §7.4 says the
> in-race migration as rears go off is **forward** — which the standing rule forbids.
> **Starting at +5 gives a live migration range of +5 → +3 → 0 that never crosses
> neutral.** On a 120-minute race at 3× wear, that is a real MFD lever he does not
> currently have from a baseline of 0.

**Next test, if a stint is spare: +3.** Not because +5 failed, but because +3 likely
keeps most of the rotation while leaving more rear margin for the end of a stint —
and it is the value the migration would pass through anyway.

# 4.4 ⭐ "Loose, especially on kerbs" — measured, and it is the DIFF

**Driver report 22 Aug: *"car felt a bit loose especially on kerbs"*.** Corroborated
and then localised in the telemetry of all six clean laps.

## The corroboration

| On kerb vs on tarmac, >80 km/h | kerb | tarmac | |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Yaw disturbance** (rad/s, frame to frame) | **0.0375** | 0.0186 | **2.02×** |
| Rear tyre slip (1.000 = rolling true) | **1.0177** | 1.0090 | rears **spinning up**, not locking |
| Frames with rear slip below 0.97 (locking) | 2.2% | 4.9% | locking is *less* common on kerbs |

**The car really is twice as unsettled on kerbs, and the rears are spinning up
rather than locking.** That makes it a traction event, not a braking one — which
already rules out brake balance and the whole §4.2 axis as the cause.

## ⭐ The decisive measurement

**Isolate the frames where exactly ONE rear wheel is on the kerb and the other is on
tarmac** — one wheel on low grip, one on high, at more than 50% throttle. 4,595
frames across six laps.

| | slip |
|---|---|
| The wheel **on the kerb** (low grip) | 1.0249 |
| The wheel **on tarmac** (high grip) | 1.0243 |
| **Difference** | **0.0006** |
| **Median L–R gap** | **0.0000** |

**An open diff would let the kerbed wheel run away from the tarmac wheel. This one
drags them to the same speed — the kerbed wheel spins the gripping wheel up with
it, and the whole axle lets go together.** Both rear wheels exceed the 1.02
wheelspin threshold in **47.9%** of kerb-and-power frames, against 21.1% on tarmac.

**That is `02` §4.1's signature, verbatim:** *"when you break traction with higher
locking numbers, the transition from having grip to losing grip with BOTH rear tires
is quite a sharp loss of traction — this is why GT7 tuners run much lower
acceleration sensitivity than real-world practice would suggest."* And `08` A5:
**"a heavily locked diff breaks away as a unit. Run acceleration sensitivity LOW."**

> **⭐ And it explains the Stavelot spin too.** That was full throttle, peak lateral
> load, brakes untouched, both rears going at once (§4.2.1). **Same mechanism, same
> axle, different corner.** Three observations now converge: the driver's report, the
> kerb telemetry, and the spin.

## The change — and it is the opposite of what was predicted

**LSD acceleration sensitivity DOWN from 18. Sweep 18 → 8 → 0.**

⚠️ **This contradicts the a-priori reasoning in `17` §9**, which argued that with
driven-wheel slip down 40–60% on v1.71 the optimum should move *up*, and `05`
§1.4's *"one of the few tracks where erring toward more acceleration lock is
correct."* **Both are reasoning; this is measurement, on this car, at this circuit.
Measurement wins — and the fact that two independent arguments pointed the other way
is exactly why §5 test 1 said to sweep the span rather than walk up from 18.**

**0 is genuinely new territory** — the old floor was 5 and v1.71 moved it to 0
(§1). Nobody has run this parameter at 0 on any car.

> **⚠️ The cost, stated up front.** Less accel lock means the inside wheel spins
> more freely out of slow corners, and `05` calls **La Source exit "the single most
> consequential traction event on any circuit in GT7."** **Watch La Source exit speed
> on every step of the sweep** — if it falls, that is the price and it has to be
> weighed against the kerb stability.

**If the diff sweep does not fix it**, the fallback is `08` A7's kerb order — rear
ride height +3–5 mm first, then rear damping. ⚠️ A7 is marked **[UNKNOWN] on 1.71**
because the patch reworked damper attenuation and suspension ranges, so it is the
weaker branch. **The diff is the branch with a measurement behind it.**

| Race, incl. 180 s extra time | ≈ 123 min → **≈ 50 laps** |
| **Fuel stints needed** | 50 ÷ 19.2 = **2.6 → minimum 2 stops, 3 stints of ≈ 17 laps** |

> ### ⭐ The decision rule this produces
>
> **Fuel forces three stints of about 17 laps whatever tyre is fitted.** So the
> right compound is **the softest one that survives 17 laps at 3×** — anything more
> durable than that is wasted, exactly as he says. Extra stops are not an attractive
> alternative here: `05` puts Spa's pit lane among GT7's longest at an estimated
> 28–32 s and says **the overcut is comparatively strong**.

## ⛔ Why the compound cannot be chosen from anything on file

**The two available reference points disagree by a factor of about eight, and
neither was measured on v1.71.**

| Source | Says | Scaled to 3× |
|---|---|---|
| `05` §1.4, Spa | RH lasts **12–16 laps at 1×** | 4–5 laps → **ten stops.** Absurd |
| In-house, Monza | RH gave **15–17 laps at 8×** | far longer than 17 → **RH massively over-durable** |

**They cannot both be right.** The most likely readings are that `05`'s per-track lap
figures are estimates rather than measurements, and that **the multiplier is not the
linear rate scalar everyone assumes** — which `CLAUDE.md` §5.2 already flags as
**assumed, never proven**, and forbids converting silently across.

**On top of that:** tyre wear is **completely unmeasured on v1.71 on every car**
(`17` §8, now six sessions running), and the RH:RM wear ratio of 1.4–1.6× that
every compound comparison leans on **has never been measured on any version**
(Rev C §3.3).

**So the honest position is that his intuition is probably right and nothing on file
can confirm it.** Four unknowns stacked on one estimate is not a compound choice.

## ⭐ The measurement that settles it, and it is one stint

**Run RM for 10 laps at 3× with the gauge read at laps 3, 6 and 10, then
extrapolate to 17.** If RM holds 17 laps, it is free pace: the Monza comparison put
RM about **2.0 s/lap** faster than RH, which over 50 laps is **100 seconds** — far
more than any setup change on this sheet is worth.

**Then fall back to RH only if RM fails**, because RH's job here is to be the
fallback rather than the default.

## Night, and why it leans the same way

The lobby runs into darkness, so track temperature falls through the race. **Cooler
track means less thermal degradation and a softer compound holding on longer**, and
a softer compound also reaches temperature more easily in the cold — both point the
same way. **⚠️ But this is reasoning, not measurement**, and GT7's post-1.49
temperature model has never been characterised in-house — no optimal window has ever
been published for GT7, by anyone. **It supports the test; it does not replace it.**
Take the gauge readings late in the session, when the light has actually gone.
