# 0. Read this first

**This event is not a setup problem. It is a fuel-and-stops problem, and the setup is built to serve it.**

Three numbers set everything:

1. **Refuel at 1 L/s.** That is five to ten times slower than GT7's normal rate. A full tank takes **100 seconds** to put in. Every litre you don't burn is a second you don't spend stationary.
2. **8× tyre wear on a track the knowledge base rates 20–25 laps at 1×.** Straight division gives **2.5–3.1 laps a stint**. The one in-house wear measurement we have (Laguna, 10 Aug) found the models **1.8× too pessimistic**, which would make it 4.5–5.6. Those two answers are a **four-stop race and an eight-stop race**. Nobody can build a strategy across that gap.
3. **Monza's slipstream is the strongest in GT7.** Track position is worth less here than anywhere. That makes an extra stop cheaper at Monza than at any other circuit — and it makes fuel-saving in a tow nearly free.

> ### ⛔ The one thing that must happen before the race
> **Run three laps at race pace, from full fuel, on the compound you intend to race, at 8×.** Read the tyre bars and the fuel counter. It takes ten minutes.
>
> This is not a suggestion — it is standing rule C3.1 in the playbook, written after skipping it at Laguna cost roughly a second a lap for a whole race. **No modelled wear figure in this knowledge base may be used to select a race compound.** Everything in §7 below is conditional on that measurement and says so.

**What the setup does about it.** Low drag, heavy kerb compliance, braking stability at neutral bias, and a deliberately free-ish diff — because at Monza less diff lock means less rear scrub, and **rear is the wear-limited axle here**. As at Laguna, the rotation fix and the tyre-life fix point the same way. That is rare and it is worth taking.

## 1.1 Two notes on reading this sheet

**Click counts on natural frequency assume 0.05 Hz steps.** GT7's NF step is 0.01–0.05 depending on car and it was not recorded when the ranges were measured. **The percentage and the absolute value are authoritative; the click count is a convenience.** Worth noting the actual step next time the settings screen is open.

**Toe is given in absolute degrees only, deliberately.** This car's measured toe range is **±1.00°**, twice the ±0.50 the reference material assumes. 5% of that range is 0.10° — twice the size of the smallest change worth making. Percent-of-range is actively dangerous on this parameter for this car.

# 2. Why each value deviates from the baseline

Values at the baseline sheet are not explained. Only the deviations, which keeps this short enough to read.

### Body height 60 / 68 (baseline: +3–5 front, +5–8 rear)

Front is at the top of the baseline band and rear one click past it. **Monza has the highest kerb severity in GT7** — the sausage kerbs at Rettifilo and Roggia will launch a stiff car and, post-1.49, land it badly. The track reference is explicit: do not run minimum, go two to four clicks up. The top-speed cost of a slightly taller car is smaller than the cost of one launched exit at Roggia.

**On rake, read this car's numbers carefully.** Its ride-height floors are **55 front / 60 rear** — the car has **+5 mm of rake built into its own minimums**. The race sheet's 60/68 is +8 mm total, i.e. only **+3 mm of rake beyond the car's natural floor**. That is deliberately modest: positive rake promotes entry rotation, and this circuit's three heaviest-in-GT7 braking events with 100 L aboard want the rear settled more than they want free rotation. Quali runs +2 mm beyond natural.

### Natural frequency 3.35 / 3.50 — and the heuristic that had to be discarded

**The baseline sheet says 70–80% of range. On this car that is 4.40–4.60 Hz and it is wrong.**

This car's measured NF floor is **3.00 Hz** — already race-stiff before you touch anything. The 70–80% rule was written for cars whose range floor is soft; percent-of-range and absolute Hz give completely different answers when the floor is high. **Anchor in absolute Hz here.**

3.35 / 3.50 is not a guess: it is **exactly what this car ran at Fuji** in race trim (3.35 / 3.45) and it is what the driver has already validated on this chassis. In percent-of-range terms it is 17.5% / 25% — the soft end, which is where Monza wants to be, and the compliance the kerbs need is bought from ride height and compression damping rather than by going softer than this car has ever been asked to run.

Rear stays above front per the baseline: softer front plus stiffer rear is rotation on a stable platform.

### ARB 5 / 3 race (baseline MR: 6 / 3–4)

Front softened one step from the MR baseline. The track reference asks for slightly softer bars than baseline at Monza specifically — **ARBs transmit single-wheel kerb inputs across the axle and are a major kerb-hop contributor**, and this circuit is nothing but kerbs. Quali goes back to 6 / 4 for roll control through the Lesmos and the Parabolica on a lap where kerb survival matters less than response.

### Damper compression 23 / 25 (baseline 28 / 30)

**Down 5 clicks both ends** — the track reference calls for 4–6 clicks off at Monza. This one is free for us: **front compression low is also the single best turn-in tool in GT7** (playbook A2), so the kerb requirement and the driver's number-one priority want the same thing. Rear stays 2 above front so the car still supports itself under the Rettifilo stop.

### Damper expansion 38 / 34 (baseline 40 / 38)

Rear expansion is down 4. Two reasons stacking: a rear that extends too fast on lift unloads abruptly, so **lower rear rebound is the rear-stability stack's #2 lever** — and a slow-rebounding damper **packs down over successive kerb strikes**, which at Roggia and Ascari means a car that gets progressively lower through the chicane. Both point the same way. Expansion still exceeds compression at both ends.

### Camber 1.0 / 1.0 race (baseline 1.2 / 1.2)

Monza is the **lowest-lateral-load circuit in the road-course set** and has the **heaviest braking demands in Gr.3**. Camber's mid-corner benefit is at its smallest here and its longitudinal cost — braking and traction — is at its most expensive. This is not the defensive wear-protection cut the playbook warns about in A1; it is paid for by the braking performance that is this driver's primary weapon. Quali goes back to 1.2 for the Parabolica.

### Front toe 0.00 race / −0.05 quali (baseline 0.00, A/B pending)

The Fuji RSR ran −0.10 to −0.15 and liked it. **The RSR high-speed rule in the driver profile says less front toe-out on fast circuits**, and there is a documented tendency for front toe beyond ±0.05° to introduce **steering oscillation at speed on some cars**. At 285 km/h into the biggest stop in Gr.3 racing, that is not a risk worth running blind. Race sits at zero; quali takes the smallest toe-out step available.

**This is test #1 in §8.** It is GT7's most disputed parameter, it sits directly on the driver's stated first priority, and it has never been A/B'd on this car.

### Rear toe +0.08 race (baseline +0.05 to +0.10)

Mid-band, deliberately. Rear toe-in is the cheapest stability there is, but it is **the largest alignment contributor to tyre wear** and it costs straight-line speed — and this is simultaneously the most speed-sensitive circuit in the game and an 8× wear event on a rear-limited track. Stability is taken from LSD braking sensitivity instead, which costs neither.

### LSD 5 / 16 / 24 race (baseline MR: 5 / 15 / 20)

**Acceleration 16.** The track reference gives 15–22 for Monza and flags it as the exception to "traction exits onto straights want more lock" — because every one of these exits is **over an aggressive kerb**, and a locked diff on a kerb strike produces snap oversteer. Take the small bog and keep the car. 16 also means less rear scrub on every exit, which is the wear currency at 8×.

**Braking 24, four above the MR baseline.** Three of the heaviest braking events in the game, a driver who trail-brakes deep, and brake balance held at neutral. This is the entire off-throttle rear-stability budget and it is being spent here rather than on brake bias or rear toe. The Laguna Huracán ran 26 successfully against a downhill stop taken while turning in — 24 on a flat, smooth, straight-line-braking circuit is well inside proven territory.

**Watch for the failure mode on the other side.** Braking sensitivity that is too high makes the car refuse to turn in on the brakes. If the Rettifilo entry feels dull, this number comes down before anything else moves — see §8.

Quali runs 18 / 22: a little more drive off the chicanes when tyre life is irrelevant, a little less brake-side lock on a light car.

### Downforce 370 / 540 race (baseline: max front, trim rear to balance)

**This is the one circuit where the standing "never trim rear first" rule is correctly suspended,** and the measured ranges show why it is safe to do it *on this car*:

| Aero setting | Total | Front share |
|---|---|---|
| Both at minimum (350 / 500) | 850 | 41.2% |
| Both at maximum (450 / 700) | 1150 | 39.1% |

**The RSR's aero window is very nearly balance-neutral** — 2 percentage points of front share across the entire diagonal, and both ends sit at GT7's typical ~40% cluster. So at Monza you can take the total downforce down without the balance shift that normally makes trimming the rear dangerous. Race sits at **20% of both ranges**; quali goes lower still.

Not at minimum, on purpose: leaving 20% of travel in both directions means the car can be trimmed either way after the first run without re-reading the range, and the race car keeps a little rear support for three 285-to-90 km/h stops with a full tank aboard.

Worth remembering the scale of what is being bought: a controlled GT7 test found **1,300 lb of downforce worth about one tenth of a second per lap**. Aero here is a balance and drag tool, not a grip tool.

### Brake balance 0

Driver preference, and it stands. The track reference suggests one to two clicks forward for T1 stability — that requirement is being met mechanically through LSD braking sensitivity and rear damping instead. **Bias is a session trim, applied last, with the in-race plan in §6.3.**

# 3. Gearing — built from the circuit, not from a top-speed number

## 3.1 The two constraints that actually set this gearbox

The track reference is unusually specific about Monza, and both of these outrank the final drive:

1. **2nd gear must cover all three chicanes without an upshift between the two apexes.** Getting 2nd right is worth more than getting the final drive right.
2. **4th must carry the Parabolica cleanly** — no shift at the apex of a long decreasing-radius corner.

And the third, which is a Monza speciality:

3. **6th tops out *with a tow*, at the Rettifilo braking board.** In clean air you should arrive 200–300 rpm short of the limiter. This is the most tow-dependent road circuit in GT7 and gearing for clean air throws away the only speed the RSR can get.

## 3.2 Two gearboxes, two different jobs

These are **not** the same box with a trimmed final drive. They are built for different races.

| | **RACE** | **QUALIFYING** |
|---|---|---|
| Compound | Racing Hard (likely) — less grip | Racing Soft — more grip |
| Traffic | Tow available, and Monza's is the strongest in GT7 | Solo lap, clean air, no tow |
| Fuel | 100 L at the start, economy is worth pit time | Light |
| Priority | Traction over kerbs at TCS 0, economy, repeatability | Outright single-lap time. Nothing else. |
| **Consequence** | **Longer 1st–3rd, longer 6th** | **Shorter 1st–3rd, shorter 6th** |

**Why the lower gears differ.** The playbook's rule is to lengthen 1st/2nd/3rd deliberately on a high-power RWD car — this chassis logged **22 wheelspin events in a single Fuji lap**. At Monza that matters more than usual because every traction exit is **over an aggressive kerb** at TCS 0. But the Laguna note qualifies it: *the long-lower-gears argument is weaker on a soft compound, because more rear grip means less wheelspin for a given gear.* Quali runs Softs with nothing to protect, so it takes the shorter gears and the extra drive off the three chicanes. The race runs Hards with a rear-limited tyre at 8× and takes the traction instead.

**Why the top gear differs.** The race 6th is geared to top out **in a tow**, so in clean air you arrive at the Rettifilo board a few hundred rpm short of the limiter — that is correct here and it is also worth fuel. A solo qualifying lap has no tow, so 6th is geared **5–6% shorter** to actually reach the limiter at the braking board.

## 3.3 The resulting speeds

| Gear | Race tops at | Quali tops at | Set by |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1st | 120 km/h | 105 km/h | Nothing. Rolling start; Monza's slowest corner is a 2nd-gear chicane. |
| 2nd | 170 | 155 | **The chicanes.** Must still pull cleanly at the exit of the second apex, all three. |
| 3rd | 214 | 199 | Lesmo 1 and Lesmo 2, both medium 3rd-gear rights. |
| 4th | 254 | 237 | **The Parabolica** — carries the corner through track-out, shift on the straight after. |
| 5th | 284 | 268 | Spacing. Curva Grande is taken here or in 6th. |
| 6th | 308 | 291 | Race: tow gear, ~285 in clean air. Quali: clean-air limiter at the T1 board. |

Increments race: +50, +44, +40, +30, +24. Quali: +50, +44, +38, +31, +23. Diminishing in both, as they should be — drag dominates the upper gears and the steps close up.

## 3.4 The procedure, in order — this is the #1 GT7 gearbox error

1. **Maximum Speed slider fully left (200 km/h) FIRST.** It is a generator, not a trim: touching it re-derives every ratio and **wipes every individual gear you have set**. Do it before anything else, on each sheet, and never touch it again.
2. **Final gear** — 3.550 race, 3.750 quali.
3. **Individual ratios** to the table in §1. Each gear's slider is clamped by its neighbours, so set them in order 1st → 6th.
4. **Trim the final gear last**, and only the final gear, per §3.5.

*If the car shows a 7-speed rather than the assumed 6, add the extra ratio between 4th and 5th and re-space the top three around it.*

## 3.5 Self-correction — one lap, one number

**These ratios are computed, not measured.** They assume an **8,800 rpm limiter** and a **2.20 m rear rolling circumference**, neither of which was recorded when the slider ranges were. If either is out, every speed above scales by the same factor and the fix is a single number.

**The check.** Run 6th to the limiter down the main straight, in a tow, and read the speed.

```
FD_corrected = FD × (speed you actually saw ÷ speed in the table)
```

Saw 318 in race trim against a 308 target → `3.550 × 318/308 = 3.665`. That one change fixes every gear at once; the individual ratios never need touching.

**And if you want the whole box exact,** read the speed at the limiter in any gear and compute the car's real constant:

```
K = observed speed × that gear's ratio × final drive
```

Then any gear's top speed is `K ÷ (ratio × final drive)`. **K = 1161.6 is what these ratios assume.** Record the real number — it makes every future gearbox on this car exact rather than iterated.

**Validate against actual pull, not the number on the screen.** At Fuji this car recorded **271 km/h against a nominal 300 km/h target**. Gearing to the nominal figure would have been exactly wrong, and it is the reason this section exists.

## 3.6 The fuel dimension

The race box's long 6th lowers stint consumption, and at 1 L/s refuel that converts directly into pit time (§4). Do not shorten it chasing acceleration on the main straight — the tow gives that back for free, and the quali box already exists for the laps where outright speed is the only thing that counts.

# 4. The fuel arithmetic — where this race is won

## 4.1 Estimated, and flagged as estimated

No Monza consumption figure exists in the knowledge base. Deriving from the one tested Gr.3 datapoint (Fuji, 3.1 L/lap at 2× → 1.55 L/lap at 1×), scaled for Monza's 27% greater distance and much higher full-throttle share (78–80%):

```
Estimated 1× consumption                    ≈ 2.0–2.3 L/lap
At 3× multiplier                            ≈ 6.0–7.0 L/lap
Race distance, 50 min at ~1:50              ≈ 27 laps
Total fuel required                         ≈ 162–190 L
Tank                                           100 L  (every car, always)
FUEL THAT MUST GO IN DURING THE RACE        ≈ 62–90 L
At 1 L/s, that is                           ≈ 62–90 SECONDS STATIONARY
```

**Confidence: low.** This is a derivation from a different circuit, not a measurement. **The in-game fuel counter over three green laps settles it in five minutes, and the diamond marker on the fuel gauge is accurate and is the authority in the race.**

## 4.2 Why this dominates everything

At a normal 5–10 L/s refuel rate, 80 L costs 8–16 seconds and nobody thinks about it. **At 1 L/s it costs 80 seconds** — roughly three-quarters of a lap, on top of pit loss and dead time. Fuel is not a strategy detail at this event; it is the largest single controllable time loss on the sheet.

## 4.3 The exchange rate, computed

**Short-shifting.** Measured at roughly **0.5 s/lap for a 20% fuel saving**.

```
Cost   0.5 s/lap × 27 laps                  = 13.5 s
Save   20% of ~175 L                        = 35 L = 35 s of refuelling
NET GAIN                                    ≈ 21 seconds
```

And it is better than that, because **short-shifting also reduces rear tyre wear** by keeping the engine out of the peak-torque region on exit — and rear is the wear-limited axle at Monza in an 8× event. **Fuel, pit time and tyre life all improve together. Do it from lap 1.**

**Slipstream saving is the free one.** Dropping to fuel map 5 or 6 while in another car's tow costs almost nothing in lap time and can save 10–15% of a stint's fuel. **Monza has the most powerful slipstream in GT7.** A driver disciplined about hitting the map button every time they are in a tow will bank more here than at any other circuit on the calendar.

**Fuel maps as a blanket setting are the weaker play.** Map 3 saves ~16% of fuel for ~8% power — worth roughly 28 seconds of refuelling against 16–22 seconds of lap time. Marginal. Short-shifting and tow-saving beat it on both counts. **Run map 1 as the default and use the map dynamically.**

## 4.4 One setup consequence, from the playbook

> *"Do not set the car up on full-power hot laps and then race it on map 4."*

If the race ends up being run substantially in a leaner map, less torque is being managed and **LSD acceleration sensitivity could come down another notch or two**. The sheet above is built for map 1 with short-shifting, which keeps full power available. That is the intended race configuration.

# 5. Tyres and stops — the conditional plan

## 5.1 The honest position

| Source | Stint at 8× | Stops in 27 laps |
|---|---|---|
| Knowledge base model (20–25 laps at 1×) | 2.5–3.1 laps | 8–10 |
| Model corrected by the one in-house measurement (÷1.8 pessimism) | 4.5–5.6 laps | 4–5 |
| Polyphony's own design pattern (4–8× forces one stop in 10–15 laps) | 7–8 laps | 3 |

**Three defensible methods, three completely different races.** This cannot be resolved by reasoning and it is not going to be. Measure it.

## 5.2 What to do with the measurement

Once you know the real stint length **L**:

| If L is… | Race shape | Compound call |
|---|---|---|
| **≤ 4 laps** | 6+ stops. A pit-stop race. | **Racing Hard.** Every extra lap per stint is worth more than the compound delta. |
| **5–7 laps** | 4–5 stops. The likely case. | **Racing Hard** for the race, and check whether RM buys a full extra lap — at Monza the RM→RH delta can be **under 0.4 s/lap**, so RH costs less here than almost anywhere. |
| **≥ 8 laps** | 3 stops, fuel and tyres roughly aligned. | **Racing Medium** becomes live. Re-measure on RM before committing. |

**Qualifying is Racing Soft regardless** — no stint to protect, and the compound delta is pure lap time.

## 5.3 Two things that are true at Monza and almost nowhere else

**An extra stop is cheap here.** Slipstream is powerful enough that a car a full second a lap slower stays attached. Track position is worth less at Monza than at any other circuit in the game. When the arithmetic is close, **take the fresher tyres** — you will tow back onto the group you rejoined behind.

**Split the fuel across the stops.** GT7 forces a full 100 L start and you cannot underfuel. But if tyres are dictating four or five stops anyway, taking **small splashes at each** rather than one big fill keeps the average fuel load — and therefore the car — lighter for the whole race, at identical total refuel time. Take what the diamond marker asks for plus about one lap of margin, no more.

## 5.4 Which tyre goes first

The circuit reference says **rear-biased, with front-right taking more than front-left** (Lesmos and the Parabolica are both rights). GT7 does not allow partial or split-axle tyre changes, so this cannot be solved in the pits — only by setup and by mid-race brake balance. It is already reflected in the low accel-LSD, the modest rear toe and the short-shifting recommendation.

**Log which corner actually goes first during the measurement run.** It decides where the remaining setup effort goes, and it is free to observe.

# 6. In-race plan

## 6.1 The stint shape

Set for **mid-stint**, per the standing principle — neither undriveable on lap 1 at 100 L nor sluggish at the end. With stints likely in the 4–7 lap range, the fuel-load swing within a stint is small, which is one genuine simplification this event hands you.

## 6.2 Fuel map plan

| Situation | Map |
|---|---|
| Default, clean air | **1**, with short-shifting |
| In a tow, anywhere on the lap | **5 or 6** — the single highest-value habit at this circuit |
| Attacking or defending | **1**, no exceptions |
| Final lap, fuel in hand | **1** |

## 6.3 Brake balance migration

Starts at **0** and mostly stays there. The MFD adjustments:

| When | Move to | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Out-lap and first flying lap | **+1** (rearward) | Monza's long straights cool the fronts, and post-1.49 the first T1 application on cooled fronts locks more readily than you expect |
| Green running | **0** | Baseline |
| Late in a stint, if the rear moves under braking | **−1** | Rear is the wear-limited axle here; protect it once it starts giving up |
| Rain | **−1 to −2** | Rear lock on a wet surface is not recoverable |

**One click, two corners, then decide.** Do not stack changes under pressure.

## 6.4 TCS — a flag, not a change

The brief specifies **TCS 0 for both quali and race** and the sheet reflects that. Two things worth having considered before the lights:

- The playbook baseline is **TCS 1 for races** — near-zero measurable lap-time cost, catches the worst exits. In an event with **8× wear, a day-to-night transition and variable weather**, all three of which degrade exit traction as the race goes on, the case for 1 is stronger than usual.
- **TCS is adjustable live via the MFD.** If it rains, go to **2** immediately. If the rear starts spinning up on the chicane exits late in a stint, **1** costs less than the moment you lose.

Your call, and 0 is defensible on a wheel with this diff. But have the button ready.

## 6.5 Day into night, and the weather

**Day → night.** The track cools through the race. Expect tyres to take **longer to come in**, which makes late out-laps materially slower than early ones — post-1.49 the cold-tyre out-lap penalty is real and it is worst on a cool surface. Two consequences: the out-lap after a late stop is not a lap to attack, and the +1 rearward brake balance on out-laps matters more at the end of the race than at the start.

**Dynamic / variable conditions.** Watch the rain radar in the MFD.

- **Err wet.** Being on wets when you needed inters costs a little; being on inters when you needed wets costs the race. The measured gap between the two at heavy rain has been as large as **7–8 s/lap**.
- **Slicks in the rain are not slow, they are unusable.** There is no heroic play available.
- **Aquaplaning is modelled as sudden and largely unwarned**, and puddles form in consistent, learnable places. If it starts raining, find out where they are at reduced pace before committing.
- You are stopping every few laps anyway. **A weather stop at this event is nearly free** — which is one more reason to react early rather than gamble.

# 7. Three things to test first

If the car is not right, in this order. **One change per run, three clean laps, and if you can't feel it, revert it.**

### 1. Front toe A/B — −0.05 / 0.00 / +0.05

Fifteen minutes, and it is the highest-value fifteen minutes available on this car. GT7's front toe behaviour is **genuinely disputed and may be inverted** relative to real-world expectation; the two leading positions agree only that **toe-in makes the front lazier**, which is the trait this driver hates most. The Fuji result favoured toe-out, but that was a slower circuit and this car has never been properly A/B'd. Run it before building anything else on top of an assumption.

### 2. If the rear moves under braking — LSD braking sensitivity up, in 2s

24 → 26 → 28. **Do not move brake balance.** This is the whole point of the rear-stability stack: the rear gets stabilised mechanically so bias can stay at neutral. If 28 is not enough, the next lever is rear expansion damping **down** (34 → 32), not bias.

### 3. If it hops or launches on the chicane kerbs — ride height first

Post-1.49 this is a ride-height problem, not a damper problem. **+3 mm both ends** (60→63, 68→71), then compression damping −2, then ARB −1. In that order.

**Diagnostic distinction that saves a session:** hops once and settles = too stiff, go to springs and bars. Bounces repeatedly for 1–3 seconds = frequency mismatch, make a **large** NF change (±0.3 Hz), not small damper tweaks. **Bangs and then won't steer = arch contact, and ride height is the only thing that fixes it.**

### ⚠️ And the trap, which is not on the list

**If the car pushes wide at Lesmo or on the Parabolica exit, ask where your right foot is before touching anything.**

- **Off throttle or trailing brake** → front mechanical grip. Front ARB 5 → 4.
- **On throttle** → **LSD acceleration sensitivity down, 16 → 14.** Not front ARB, not front downforce.

This exact fork cost a test session at Laguna. Front ARB and front downforce were both queued as fallbacks there and both would have masked a diff problem while giving up roll control. The corner that is **not** complaining is diagnostic — note it every time.

**Two values sit at a hard limit, both deliberately:**

**LSD initial torque = 5, the minimum.** This is the baseline value for every layout in the playbook and the reason is specific: **high preload is a silent cause of the persistent mid-corner push this driver dislikes most**, and it is always-on — it costs turn-in everywhere on the lap, not just where you want stability. There is no case for lifting it here. If the car ever feels darty in a straight line, +2 is the response, but Monza is not where that shows up.

**Max speed = 200 km/h, the minimum.** This is not a setting, it is step one of the transmission flip procedure — pushing the generator fully left produces the tightest possible spacing, which the final gear then stretches into a set that is simultaneously close-ratio and long-legged. **The Max Speed slider's value is meaningless once the individual ratios are set.** What matters is the per-gear speed table in §3.2.

**Individual gear ratios have no fixed endpoints to check against.** GT7 clamps each gear by its neighbours rather than by a stored min/max, so a ratio is legal as long as it sits between the gears either side of it. Both sets are monotonic with comfortable gaps, so neither will hit a clamp — but set them **in order, 1st through 6th**, because setting a middle gear before its neighbours can bump against a clamp that disappears a moment later.

**The gearbox is the one part of this sheet that is computed rather than measured.** The ratios assume an 8,800 rpm limiter and a 2.20 m rear rolling circumference. The final drives are comfortably mid-range with room in both directions, and **§3.5 corrects the entire box from a single observed speed** — so an error here costs one lap of validation, not a rebuild.

# 9. What to record

Per the standing rule, everything measured comes back into the knowledge base with a date and a version.

1. **Tyre stint length at 8×**, per compound — the number this entire strategy is missing.
2. **Fuel consumption per lap at 3×**, map 1, with and without short-shifting.
3. **Which tyre goes first**, and at what lap.
4. **Actual top speed** in clean air and in a tow, which gear you are in at each corner exit, and — the valuable one — **the car's gearing constant K** from §3.5. One reading makes every future gearbox on this car exact instead of iterated. Note the **limiter rpm** while you are at it.
5. **The front toe A/B result** — and the NF step size while you are in the settings screen.
