# Fuji, 24 Aug 2026 — the first race measured on v1.71

**Session 88, event 6** (Lamborghini Huracán GT3 '15, Fuji International Full
Course, Racing Soft, 20 laps, 2× tyre wear, 3× fuel, one mandatory stop).
**P5 from P10 at the first crossing.** Green 20:18:25 → flag 20:52:42, 2,057 s.

Every figure below is re-derived from `data/pitcrew.db` and the stored 60 Hz
frames, and each was put through an adversarial review before it was written
here. **Six claims were refuted in that review and are not in this document;
three more were withheld as resting on a broken instrument.** What survived is
marked with its source class, because most of it is derived rather than read.

> **Scope warning.** `grip_observations` holds **zero rows for this session** and
> there is no Fuji tyre model at any compound — the derived-metrics pipeline has
> not run since 18 Aug and every fitted model in the database is stamped v1.70.
> So the app measured almost none of this; it was re-derived by hand. That is a
> gap in the *measuring* half, recorded here because it bounds what the
> reasoning half may lean on.

---

## 1. The driver beat the engineer again, and that is now the pattern

The approved plan was **three stops**, approved eight seconds before the green.
The app's own optimiser priced it **50.0 s slower** than the one-stop candidate
it replaced. He stopped once, on lap 5, ignored box calls on laps 10 and 11, and
ran to the flag. On the third call the app folded and agreed with him — the only
one of twenty-five revisions marked `accepted`.

**Four consecutive sessions in which his judgement beat the app's.** This is now
a standing fact about how to weight the two, not an anecdote.

The cause was not the optimiser. Stint length is the lowest of three ceilings —
tyre 20.7 laps, tank 15.4, and **the longest stint anyone had actually run,
which was 6**. Evidence bound it, and the plan reported the constraint as
*fuel*. Those two readings demand opposite driving: *fuel-limited* means staying
out is not available; *evidence-limited* means nobody has tried yet.

> **The action that removes this is a practice run, not a code change.** One
> deliberate 12–15 lap stint lifts the cap, changes the plan, and produces a real
> degradation curve. It is the cheapest item on the list and it needs no app work
> at all.

**σ 0.817 s puts the lap-time degradation detection floor at ≈1.6 s/lap**, above
the whole 0.5–1.5 s/lap band `03` describes. The standing rule holds here as
everywhere: **lap time confirms degradation, it can never warn.** Yas is 2.04,
Monza ~0.70, Watkins 0.68 — but Watkins is v1.70 and this is v1.71, so the
comparison is indicative only.

### The burn rose as the car lightened
Rows 6–12 mean **5.936 L/lap** → rows 13–19 mean **6.313 L/lap**, a **6.4%
increase** across one stint. Worth knowing before sizing a fill off an early-
stint figure.

### The plan's burn was 6.7% rich, and the pooling is why
Plan 13 used 6.507 L/lap against 6.098 actual. The practice session it pooled
from includes session 87, which is `practice_intent='qualifying'` — a
qualifying run pooled into a race fuel model. The earlier plan, built before
that session existed, used 6.246 and was closer.

## 3. The pit stop, decomposed — and the declared numbers are wrong

Total stationary **50.68 s**; total cost against a green lap **57.5 s**.

| Component | Measured | Declared / modelled |
|---|---|---|
| Refuelling | 28.62 s (28.642 L) | — |
| Dead time before fuel flows | **22.05 s** | 7.5 s |
| Pit-lane transit | **6.8 s** | 20.0 s |
| **Total** | **57.5 s** | ~56 s |

**The total is right for the wrong reasons and the errors nearly cancel.**
Transit is over-declared by 13 s and dead time under-modelled by 15 s. They will
not cancel on a splash-and-dash, where there is little fuel time to hide in.

> **The Fuji dead time includes a tyre change.** Watkins measured 6.79 s and may
> not have. Do not treat the two as the same constant, and do not carry either
> to a third circuit — `03`'s rule that pit loss is a track constant still
> stands, but its *decomposition* is not.

### He crossed the line with 8.276 L aboard
That is **8.3 s stationary** at the measured rate, and roughly ten times what
his own burn scatter justifies (sd of a 14-lap total ≈ 0.79 L). **It is not
chargeable to him.** The only fuel figure he was given in the box —
*"71 litres aboard, that already covers it"* — was computed for the three-stop
plan, at the exact second fuel began flowing, and the app had no path that
produced a to-the-flag number at the box at all. Filling the tank was the
rational response to being handed a figure for a race he was not driving.

This is the same standing refusal `driver.md` already records: **he will not
carry a lap of spare fuel, and any conservatism must be priced in seconds and
said out loud.**

## 4. Tyres: the one instrument that moved was temperature

**No degradation was detectable in lap time or in lateral grip over a fifteen-
lap stint.** That is *not* the same as "the tyres did not degrade", and the
distinction is the whole finding:

- Lap time on lap-in-stint, n=14: slope **−0.064 ± 0.041 s/lap** — the sign is
  *faster*. Removing the fuel-weight term (94 → 8 L at the derived, never
  measured, 0.003 s/L/lap) leaves −0.045 ± 0.041, t = −1.12.
- The null bounds *average* degradation below ≈0.03 s/lap. But `03` forbids a
  linear model, and against the real piecewise shape — signal confined to the
  last laps as the set passes half worn — **the smallest end-of-stint step this
  race could have detected is ≈0.8 s/lap**, which sits inside the band it was
  meant to catch.

**So it rules out a large late collapse and rules out nothing in the smaller
half of the band. It does not refute the planned 0.04115/lap.**

### Temperature did move, and it is the only corroborator that produced a signal
Over the second stint (n=14): front mean **+0.163 °C/lap** (t = 6.17), rear mean
**+0.312 °C/lap** (t = 8.33), and the **rear-minus-front gap widened
+0.149 °C/lap** (t = 4.77). Rears peaked at **77.5 °C**, comfortably under the
RS wear-onset threshold of 88 °C.

Confounded by fuel burn-off, track evolution and driving, so **it is not wear**.
But `03` names temperature trend and front/rear asymmetry as one of the three
wear corroborators, and it is the only one that said anything here.

### Fresh tyres arrived at 60.0 °C — a third value
All four surface temperatures converge to exactly 60.0 °C in a single frame at
20:27:18. Previously recorded arrival values are 45 °C and exactly 70 °C.
**Three different values across three sessions.** Any fixed fresh-tyre
temperature constant is fabricated, and nothing may be gated on one.

### No wear was measured in this race at all
Every wear column is null on all nineteen rows. The gauge reader made **553
attempts after the green and accepted none**. The rate the whole strategy rested
on — 0.04115/lap — is the plain mean of two *practice* readings taken on tyres
that were not the ones he raced, and it is serialised in the plan as
`"source": "measured"`.

## 5. Racecraft: clean, and flat

Two off-track excursions, 2.48 s on the opening lap and 0.32 s on lap 3. Zero
spins, zero crawls. Across every green lap: brake-applied fraction **16.0–18.6%**,
full throttle **56.3–58.7%**, kerb use **0.6–3.6%**. **That flatness over twenty
laps is the finding** — there is no fatigue signature and no late-race change in
his inputs. Lap 3 is the only lap where an error and a lap-time loss coincide.

> **A stability read was attempted and is deliberately absent.** The countersteer
> index available for this race reads exactly 0.00% on 14 of 19 laps against
> ~2,400 candidate frames each — it cannot distinguish *settled* from *not
> firing*, and it is not the estimator that produced the 0.0–0.2 / 0.4–0.5%
> reference bands. **No instrument in this race can speak to car stability.**

### He shifts well above every reference the app holds
Measured over 266 upshifts in the second stint:

| Upshift | Median rpm | n |
|---|---|---|
| 1→2 | **8,224** | 38 |
| 2→3 | 8,142 | 84 |
| 3→4 | 8,096 | 59 |
| 4→5 | 8,026 | 56 |
| 5→6 | 7,968 | 29 |

Rev limiter active on 1.57% of frames. The app holds **three** shift references
and none of them is what he did: the sheet says a flat 7,800 in all six gears,
the beep setting says 7,500, and the per-car table says 7,800.

**This is a pooled, whole-gearbox aggregate with n ≥ 29 per gear** — a
multi-lap trend, not per-corner input coaching, so it is inside what the charter
permits. It supports one recommendation: set the beep per gear from measurement
rather than from a flat default.

## 6. Setup: Watkins Rev D → Fuji v1, and what it is safe to conclude

Seven of twenty-two parameters moved. Dampers, anti-roll bars, natural
frequencies, the entire differential, rear camber, front toe and final drive are
identical — **this is the Watkins car with an aero and geometry trim, not a new
build.**

| Parameter | Watkins | Fuji | Δ % of range |
|---|---|---|---|
| Front downforce | 435 | 395 | **−40.0%** |
| Rear downforce | 660 | 620 | **−20.0%** |
| Ride height, front | 68 | 63 | −20.0% |
| Ride height, rear | 75 | 72 | −10.0% |
| Camber, front | 1.40 | 2.40 | +16.7% |
| Brake balance | 0 | +1 | +10.0% |
| Toe, rear | 0.08 | 0.20 | +6.0% |

**Equal absolute aero cuts are not equal cuts.** The front range is 100 wide and
the rear is 200, so −40 at both ends took **40% of the front range and 20% of
the rear** — total downforce down substantially *and the aero balance moved
rearward*, which is the opposite of what "front trimmed, rear held" implies.
Meanwhile front camber and brake bias both moved to add front bite at *low*
speed. The two ends of the corner-speed range were moved in opposite directions.
Rake increased 2 mm even though both ends came down.

### What cannot be concluded, and why

1. **Different physics.** Watkins ran v1.70 and Fuji v1.71, and that update
   changed suspension, differential and aero *ranges* as well as defaults. Only
   one range record exists per car and all are v1.71, so the Watkins column above
   is *"what those numbers would mean today"*, not what they meant then. The
   v1.70 ranges were never recorded and are unrecoverable.
2. **Ballast is unknown at Watkins** — the sheet records three empty objects. A
   15 kg mass and 26-unit CG move would swamp every effect in that table.
3. **A physics prediction was made and is not reported.** "Pointy slow,
   understeery fast" follows from the table, and it is untestable on this
   record: the per-corner metrics are empty for this session and Fuji's corner
   model is auto-segmented, so corner identity is not comparable to Watkins. It
   stays a hypothesis.

### The provenance finding, and it reverses an earlier conclusion
The sheet linked to the Watkins **race** was created four days *after* it, which
looks like a retrospective reconstruction — and the three Watkins practices point
at a different sheet that disagrees on ride height by 3 mm.

**The gear ratios settle it, and the other way round.** `laps.gear_ratios` is
decoded from the packet, not copied from a sheet: session 49 reports 6th =
1.055, matching Rev D **to 9.5e-08** and missing the other sheet by 5.0e-03. So
do all three practices. **Rev D was genuinely in the car; the practices are the
mis-linked sessions, not the race.** Suspension height corroborates: the
difference between the race and the practices is the same size as the
practice-to-practice control, and 6–12× smaller than the 3 mm the two sheets
disagree about. **The car was not raised.**

At Fuji the record is wrong at the *other* end: practice 84 points at the race
sheet but its telemetry reports the old Watkins ratios — the re-cut landed
between 16:15 and 16:37.

> **Both ends of this comparison have a broken provenance chain**, and
> `setup_changes` still holds zero rows, so no change is recorded as an
> experiment with a hypothesis and an outcome. This is `CLAUDE.md`'s rank-zero
> failure mode — *"what is actually in the car"* — and the gear-ratio channel is
> the cheapest independent witness available for it.

- **`03` tyre/fuel model:** a third fresh-tyre arrival temperature (60 °C). Any
  fixed constant is fabricated. Fuji RS shows no resolvable degradation over 15
  laps at 2×, with the detection floor stated above.
- **`05` track reference:** Fuji pit-loss decomposition — 22 s dead time with a
  tyre change, 6.8 s transit — and the warning not to carry either figure.
- **`08` playbook:** σ 0.817 s for this car and circuit; burn 6.098 L/lap at 3×,
  rising 6.4% across a stint; measured per-gear upshift points.
- **`driver.md`:** the fuel-margin refusal is reaffirmed, and the 8.3 s is
  charged to the app rather than to him.
- **`RECONCILIATION.md`:** the Watkins setup-provenance conclusion is reversed —
  see §6.
